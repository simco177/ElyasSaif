Job Market Paper

How Predictable is Household Financial Distress?

I examine the ex-ante predictability of consumer financial distress (CFD) using detailed nationally representative Finnish household-level data over 2008-2017. Unlike studies that focus on consumer default, this predictive study uses machine learning methods (ML) to analyse the predictability of CFD from the perspective of the consumer rather than the lenders. This study provides insights into the overall accuracy of ML in predicting CFD, revealing significant variability in predictability between households with and without prior experience of distress. Specifically, I find that predictability is nearly perfect for households with prior distress but significantly lower for those without. This demonstrates that predictions of CFD can accurately target support measures for households most likely to experience persistent consumer financial distress. The findings of this study hence contribute to the literature on consumer financial protection — which focuses on causally identifying measures that mitigate suboptimal borrowing decisions and consumer financial distress — by considering the predictive dimension that complements this causal focus. With more accurate predictions of CFD, consumer protection measures, such as advice how to cope with CFD, can be targeted to households that are at greatest risk.
