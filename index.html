Job Market Paper

How Predictable is Household Financial Distress?

I use machine learning (ML) methods to examine the forward-looking predictability of consumer financial distress using nationally representative Finnish household-level data. This predictive study draws parallels with the literature on consumer default predictability but is more aligned with the perspective of the consumer rather than the lender. My study contributes to the literature on consumer financial protection — which focuses on causally identifying measures that mitigate suboptimal borrowing decisions and consumer financial distress — by presenting a predictive dimension that complements this causal focus. With accurate predictions, consumer protection measures can be targeted to households at risk of consumer financial distress. This study provides insights into the overall accuracy of ML in predicting consumer financial distress, revealing significant heterogeneity in predictability between households with and without prior experience of distress. In a budget-constrained financial counselling context, predictability is nearly perfect for households with prior distress but significantly lower for those without. This demonstrates that predictions of consumer financial distress can accurately target support measures for households most likely to experience persistent consumer financial distress.
